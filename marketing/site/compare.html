Where Offhook fits, and where it doesn't.

iPhone terminals are a crowded shelf, and several of them are very good at things Offhook does not attempt. This page is the version of that comparison we would want to read before paying for something: capability by capability, with the cases where you should buy a competitor instead stated plainly rather than buried.

The short version

Offhook is built for one job: your coding agents can reach you. An agent blocks on a permission prompt, your lock screen says so, and you answer it there. Everything else — SSH, Mosh, tmux, herdr — exists so that job works on a real terminal instead of a toy one. If that is not your job, a different app is probably a better buy, and the last section says which.

When you should not choose Offhook

These are real, and they are the reason to read this page rather than the front one. If any of them is your situation, one of the apps above is the better purchase and we would rather you bought it than returned ours.

  • You want a mature product with years of reviews. Offhook is new. There is no track record to check, no long changelog, and no community answering questions at 2am. Termius, Blink, Prompt and Secure ShellFish have all been shipping for years.
  • You need file management as a first-class feature. Offhook is a terminal. If your day is SFTP, editing remote files in a GUI, or mounting a server into the Files app, Secure ShellFish is built precisely for that and Offhook is not.
  • You want the same subscription to cover desktop. Offhook is iPhone and iPad only, bought once, with no Mac or web client. Termius and Prompt cover several platforms under one account.
  • You need Android. There is no Android version and none planned.
  • You want an app that syncs hosts and keys across devices for you. Offhook has no account and no cloud, which is the point — but it also means moving to a new phone means adding your hosts again.
  • You do not run agents. If you just want a solid SSH client, everything Offhook is unusual at is wasted on you, and the one-time tmux clients are cheaper and perfectly good.

When it is clearly the right one

  • You run Claude Code, Codex or similar on your own machines, and you are tired of finding an agent that stopped hours ago waiting for a yes.
  • You use herdr, or you would if a phone client supported it.
  • You work from trains, cafés and other places where the network moves — Mosh is built in, not an add-on.
  • You would rather buy something once than rent it, and you would rather an app collected nothing about you at all.
